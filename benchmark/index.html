How the benchmark works

One photo is compressed with a truncated SVD on each colour channel. Every rank k is judged three ways, by people and by models, so each task gets a human threshold and a machine threshold.

Human side

Part 1, ascending reveal (method of limits). The photo starts at k = 1 and gains layers step by step up to k = 50. At each step you say whether you see only blobs, a face, or a face you could recognise again. The first k for each answer is your threshold. You do not see the original before this part.

Part 2, two-alternative forced choice. The original and a compressed copy are shown side by side in random order for k = 10, 20, 30, 50, 75, 100, 150 and 200, three times each (24 shuffled pairs). Accuracy near 0.5 means people can no longer tell them apart. The human threshold is the first k where group accuracy drops to 75% or below.

Machine side

Detection: SCRFD-500M face detector (InsightFace), threshold 0.5. Identity: ArcFace MobileFaceNet embeddings, cosine similarity between the compressed and the original face, both aligned with the original’s five landmarks; threshold 0.40, a common verification operating point. Fidelity: SSIM, threshold 0.95. The numbers come from scripts/machine_benchmark.py.

Known limits

One photo, one subject. Part 1 answers are self-reports, so “I’d recognise them” is a judgement, not a verified identification. Screen size changes Part 2 a lot, so each run records the display width. Anyone who has seen the photo before should tick the box at the start; those runs are kept but left out of the human thresholds.
